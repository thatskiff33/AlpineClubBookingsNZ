- A booking that once went to the office to have a change priced by hand no
  longer has to go back every single time it changes. Until now nothing could
  ever fill in a night whose price the club does not know, so a booking that was
  sent for review once was sent for review forever, and the office priced the
  same stay by hand again and again.
- When the office settles one of these reviews, the screen now lists the nights
  with no price on file and asks what each one sold for. Say what they were, and
  that guest stops sending the booking back. A booking can have more than one
  guest whose nights could not be read, and each gets its own row here — so the
  message after settling says the guest was dealt with rather than promising the
  whole booking is finished with.
- The same question is asked when a review is closed with **no adjustment**, not
  only when money moves. A change that turns out to owe nothing is one of the
  commonest ones to have this problem, and if only a settlement could fix it,
  exactly those bookings would keep coming back forever.
- **Nothing is ever filled in for you** - not from the amount being settled, not
  from an even share, not from today's rates. There is no button that produces a
  figure, because a price nobody decided is the whole thing this review exists
  to avoid. Fill in every night or leave them all blank; a half-answered set is
  not accepted, because the missing ones would have to be invented.
- The figures have to add up to what that guest's stay is recorded as being
  worth, adjusted by whatever is being settled - less if the club is paying the
  member back, more if the member is being asked for money. The box says what
  they come to and what they need to come to, and leaves the officer to close
  the gap.
- **$0.00 is a real answer for a night** that was genuinely free, and it stays
  different from leaving the box empty, which means nobody knows.
- Leaving them all blank still settles the review exactly as before. That is the
  right answer when the amount being settled is not simply what the nights were
  worth - a change fee, or a refund reduced by the club's policy - because then
  no honest set of figures would add up. **The screen now says that where it
  matters**: the sentence an officer gets when their figures do not add up names
  clearing every box as a real third option, instead of leaving "change a night's
  price" as the only way to make the arithmetic work. On a settlement like that
  the only figure which satisfies the check is a false one, and steering somebody
  towards typing it would put back exactly the invented number this work removes.
- If a box holds something that is not an amount - `1,200.00`, `$45`, a stray
  letter - the screen now says which night it cannot read, instead of telling the
  officer to fill in every night while every night visibly holds a figure.
- A price already on file can never be overwritten by this screen, and if the
  booking changes underneath the officer while they are recording the figures,
  nothing is saved and they are told to reload rather than one of the two
  answers quietly winning.
- Recording the amounts is written into the club's audit record as its own
  entry: what each night was set to, and what the guest's stay was worth before
  and after.
- Both of those sentences, and the running total, are announced to a screen
  reader and attached to the box they are about. The confirm button is disabled
  behind them, so somebody who could not see them was left with a control that
  would not press and no reason given. The note that a free night is `0.00` is
  now attached to every night box rather than only the first.
- Settling a review at **$0.00** is still refused - "we looked and nothing is
  owed" has its own button, and once a zero is recorded the two become
  impossible to tell apart. What is new is that the screen now says so, and
  names the button to use instead, at the moment the officer types the zero.
  Before this the button simply would not press and nothing explained why.
