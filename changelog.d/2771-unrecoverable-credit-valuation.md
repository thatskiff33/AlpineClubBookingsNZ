- **A night given back on an old booking is now credited at what that member
  actually paid on average, not at today's price list (#2771).** When an officer
  removes a guest from a stay that is already under way, or pulls the check-out
  back, the club hands money back for the nights being surrendered. For most
  bookings the system knows exactly what each night was sold for and returns
  precisely that. For a handful of older bookings — ones made before the system
  recorded a price against each night, and ones created by approving a booking
  request — there is no per-night record to return, and the credit used to be
  worked out from today's rates instead.

  That was wrong in both directions. If the rate had gone UP since the booking
  was made, the club handed back more than it had ever charged; if it had gone
  DOWN, the member was short-changed. Those nights are now valued from the
  member's own booking: what they paid, spread evenly across the nights they
  hold. Give back every night and the club returns exactly what was taken, never
  a cent more — which also means the safety net that used to cut an over-large
  credit back to zero, sometimes wiping out a member's whole payment for
  surrendering part of a stay, is no longer reached on these bookings.

  One trade-off, stated plainly: where such a booking spans a rate change, the
  credit is the member's average rather than that particular night's rate, so
  giving back a peak night out of a mostly off-peak stay returns the average.
  The club's own records cannot say which night was dearer, and the average is
  the number the system already stores for those nights.

  Nothing already charged, refunded, invoiced or credited is recalculated, and
  nights an edit BUYS still cost today's rate. No action is needed from anyone.
