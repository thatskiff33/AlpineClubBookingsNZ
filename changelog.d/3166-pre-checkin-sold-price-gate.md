- Changing a booking that has **not started yet** is now held to the same rule
  as changing one that is already under way: the club never works out a night's
  historical price by applying today's rates to it. This is the change most
  members make, so until now it was the common case that got the wrong answer —
  the same booking, with the same gap in its records, was handled correctly the
  day after check-in and incorrectly the day before.
- The reason it mattered beyond one edit: the guessed figures were **written
  into the booking's own price history**, where the next change read them back
  as though they were what the member had really paid. Each edit made the record
  a little less true, and nothing ever flagged it.
- Where the club cannot work the amount out, nothing is refused. The change to
  the stay is saved exactly as asked, and only the money waits: no charge, no
  refund, no credit and no change fee until someone from the office confirms the
  figure with the member. The price shown before saving says so, instead of
  quoting a number the club would not have honoured.
- Every night whose price the club does know keeps it to the cent. Only the
  nights it genuinely cannot account for are recorded as **not known** — never
  as `$0.00`, and never by deleting the record of that night.
- Moving a booking's dates no longer quietly fills in a night whose price the
  club had already recorded as **not known**. That was the one route by which
  "we do not know" could turn back into a number nobody had decided, and it
  opened as soon as an earlier review was settled. **No part of the system
  repairs a blank price by re-pricing it** — only a person supplying the amount
  can clear one.
- Adding a person to a booking whose price history cannot be read no longer
  bills the member for somebody else's night. The new arrival is still priced
  and charged normally; what stopped is the rest of the booking being silently
  re-valued at today's rates and the difference added to the bill.
- Where a person is taken off a booking whose money is held for review, the
  price history the club **did** hold for them is recorded before their rows are
  removed, so nothing the system could have known is lost.
- One consequence worth knowing: once a booking has been through this, every
  later change to it also waits for the office, because settling a review
  records the amount against the review rather than against the individual
  nights. The club is asked each time rather than told a figure nobody decided.
