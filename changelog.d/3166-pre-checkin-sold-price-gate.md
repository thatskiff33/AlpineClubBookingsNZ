- Changing a booking that has **not started yet** is now held to the same rule
  as changing one that is already under way: the club never works out a night's
  historical price by applying today's rates to it. This is the change most
  members make, so until now it was the common case that got the wrong answer —
  the same booking, with the same gap in its records, was handled correctly the
  day after check-in and incorrectly the day before.
- The reason it mattered beyond one edit: the guessed figures were **written
  into the booking's own price history**, where the next change read them back
  as though they were what the member had really paid. Each edit made the record
  a little less true, and nothing ever flagged it.
- Where the club cannot work the amount out, the change itself still goes
  through. The stay is saved exactly as asked, and only the money waits: no
  charge, no refund, no credit and no change fee until someone from the office
  confirms the figure with the member. The price shown before saving says so,
  instead of quoting a number the club would not have honoured.
- **What that costs, stated plainly: the NEXT price-affecting change to that
  booking is turned away until the office has finished.** A second change would
  have to start from the very amount nobody has worked out yet, so it is refused
  rather than guessed at. Changes that do not touch the price — correcting a
  name, for instance — still go through as normal. Bookings made before the club
  started keeping per-night prices at all are in this position from their first
  change onwards, so those members will need the office for each one.
- Every night whose price the club does know keeps it to the cent. Only the
  nights it genuinely cannot account for are recorded as **not known** — never
  as `$0.00`, and never by deleting the record of that night.
- Moving a booking's dates no longer quietly fills in a night whose price the
  club had already recorded as **not known**. That was the one route by which
  "we do not know" could turn back into a number nobody had decided, and it
  opened as soon as an earlier review was settled. **Nothing in the system
  turns a "not known" back into a figure by re-pricing it** — only a person
  supplying the amount can clear one. The waitlist offer, which re-prices a whole
  stay at today's rates when a spot comes free, now leaves such a booking at its
  last agreed price rather than writing over the gap. Approving a booking request
  still sets a price and needs no such fence: a booking held while a request is
  being considered cannot be changed at all, so it can never have a gap in it.
- Adding a person to a booking whose price history cannot be read no longer
  bills the member for somebody else's night. The new arrival is still priced
  and charged normally; what stopped is the rest of the booking being silently
  re-valued at today's rates and the difference added to the bill.
- Whenever money is held for review, the price history the club **did** hold for
  everyone else on the booking is written down first — whether they are being
  taken off it, having nights taken away, or having nights added. Each of those
  destroys the record in its own way, and none of them was recoverable
  afterwards, so nothing the system could have known is lost now.
- A second, later change of the same kind to the same booking now raises its own
  review. Previously, once the office had settled one, an identical change made
  afterwards was treated as a repeat of it: nothing was charged, nothing was
  asked, and nobody was told.
- Where the club is still working the money out, the person handling it is now
  told what else the same change did — how many people were added and what they
  were priced at. Before, the review card described one guest nobody had touched
  and said nothing about the new arrivals the booking had not been charged for.
- Two things this deliberately does **not** change, so nobody is surprised by
  them: a member can still be asked to pay, and can still pay, the price the
  booking carried before the change, with the difference settled by the office
  afterwards — that is what the review is for, and blocking payment would leave
  a member unable to pay for a booking at all. And correcting a placeholder into
  a real member still applies the name and membership immediately while the
  price waits for the office.
- One consequence worth knowing: once a booking has been through this, every
  later change to it also waits for the office, because settling a review
  records the amount against the review rather than against the individual
  nights. The club is asked each time rather than told a figure nobody decided.
